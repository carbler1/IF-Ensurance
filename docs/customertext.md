# Customer text for each risk

Status: draft, 2026-10-17. Task 4 of `docs/rasitasks.md`. Author: Rasi (analyst).

## Summary

This document holds the customer text for each risk: "why it matters" and "what you gain". It covers the 20 risks in the catalogue, 37 of the 38 new risks in `docs/cataloguereview.md`, and 2 housing company items. The 38th risk, sockets without an earth conductor, is merged into "Electrical installation not checked in 15 years" (see the interview spec), so it has no row of its own. Antton copies the text into the catalogue entries.

The text replaces the DRAFT hints in `data/catalogue/`.

## 1. Writing rules

- The reader is an average Finnish person, including an older person. The app speaks to the reader as "you".
- Each text has 2 to 4 short sentences. Each sentence has one idea.
- Use plain words. Define each term the first time, in brackets.
- Why it matters: say what can go wrong, then what it costs or harms. Do not blame the customer.
- What you gain: say what the customer gets. Do not promise a premium cut or a score. The app shows those.
- The text must stay true for every house that triggers the risk. It does not name a year or a material that only some houses have.
- No fear words. Do not use "danger" when "risk" is enough.

## 2. Electricity

| Risk | Why it matters | What you gain |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical installation not checked in 15 years | Wires age. Cables turn brittle and joints come loose. This can start a fire or give you an electric shock. A check also finds sockets that have no earthing (a safe path for stray current). | An electrician finds weak cables and missing earthing before they cause harm. You get a report for your records. |
| Old screw-fuse panel | Your fuse box is old. It uses screw-in fuses. The fuse holders can come loose and get hot. A hot holder can start a fire. | An electrician checks the panel and can replace it. You get modern fuses and a safety switch. |
| Electric sauna stove wiring not checked in 10 years | A sauna stove uses a lot of power. Old wires and an old thermostat can overheat. Sauna stoves are one of the main causes of electrical fires. | A wiring check finds a worn cable or a stuck thermostat before it starts a fire. |
| Wet-room sockets without a residual-current device | A residual-current device (RCD) is a safety switch. It cuts the power in a fraction of a second when current leaks. Without it, a fault in the bathroom can give you a shock. | A small panel job protects you in the bathroom, the sauna and the yard. |
| No surge protection | A voltage spike from a storm or a fault can break your TV, computer and other devices. A surge protector at the fuse box stops most spikes. | Your devices stay safe. You avoid repair costs and lost data. |
| EV charger or e-bike charging not checked | A charger runs for hours, often at night. A wrong cable or an old circuit can overheat. Charging near a bed, a sofa or curtains can start a fire. | An electrician checks the charging place and the circuit. You charge with less fire risk. |

## 3. Fire safety

| Risk | Why it matters | What you gain |
|---|---|---|
| Too few or old smoke alarms | A fire can fill a home with smoke in minutes. Many homes have too few alarms, old alarms or alarms that do not sound together. | Enough working alarms wake you early. This is a cheap way to protect your life. |
| No stove guard (liesivahti) | A forgotten pan on a hot stove is a common cause of home fires. A stove guard cuts the power when it senses danger. | A stove guard switches off the stove before a fire starts. You can cook with less worry. |
| No extinguisher or fire blanket | A small fire is easy to stop in the first minute. After that it grows fast. Without an extinguisher or a fire blanket, you lose that minute. | You can stop a small fire yourself and protect your home. |
| Gas or fuel stored in a basement or living space | Gas bottles and fuel can explode or burn fast. A basement or a living space is the worst place to keep them. | Safe storage lowers the risk of an explosion or a fast fire. |
| Fire barrier between row house units | In a row house, a wall and the attic keep fire from spreading to your neighbours, and from them to you. Holes from building work can break this protection. | You learn if the fire wall is whole. If it is not, you can ask the housing company to repair it. |

## 4. Heating

| Risk | Why it matters | What you gain |
|---|---|---|
| Chimney not swept in the last year | Soot builds up inside the chimney. Soot can catch fire. The law asks for a chimney sweep every year. | A yearly sweep prevents chimney fires and keeps smoke out of your home. |
| Steel oil tank over 30 years old | Steel tanks rust from the bottom. A leak puts oil in the ground. Cleaning oil from soil costs a lot. Your tank is old, or we do not know its age. | An inspection shows if the tank is safe. A new tank, or a switch to another heating system, removes the risk of a costly oil leak. |
| Oil boiler near the end of its service life | An oil boiler lasts about 25 to 30 years. An old boiler can break in winter or leak oil. | A check tells you when to replace it. You avoid a cold house and a surprise cost. |
| Old radiators with surface rust | Rust on a radiator can mean it will leak soon. A leak can wet your floor. | You replace a rusty radiator before it leaks and damages the floor. |
| Oil tank room without a fire door or protection basin | Oil burns hot. A fire door keeps flames out of the tank room. A basin (a tray under the tank) holds the oil if the tank leaks. | The tank room is safer against fire and oil leaks. |
| Heat pump or ground system not serviced | A heat pump needs a regular service. Without it, the pump uses more power, wears out faster and can fail in winter. | A service keeps your heating cost low and prevents a breakdown in cold weather. |
| No backup heat in a winter power cut | A winter power cut can last for hours. If your heating needs power, your home gets cold. Water pipes can freeze. | A backup heat source keeps your home warm and your pipes safe in a power cut. |
| Water pipes in unheated rooms | Water pipes in a cold room, a garage or a crawl space can freeze and burst. The pipe floods your home when the ice melts. | Insulation or a heating cable stops the pipes from freezing. You avoid a winter flood. |

## 5. Moisture

| Risk | Why it matters | What you gain |
|---|---|---|
| Wet room without modern waterproofing | Older wet rooms often have no waterproof layer under the tiles. Shower water then soaks into the floor and walls. The damage stays hidden for years. | A new waterproof layer keeps water out of your floor. It prevents one of the most costly damages in a home. |
| False plinth risk structure not inspected | Many houses from 1960 to 1989 have a low plinth (the base of the wall). It can hide the wooden frame near the ground. The wood can rot with no sign inside the home. | A small inspection hole shows if the frame is dry. You can fix a small problem now and avoid a big repair later. |
| Crawl space or basement not inspected for moisture | Moisture collects under and around old houses. It can lead to mould in the floor. Nobody has checked your home for it in the last 5 years. | An inspection finds damp early. You protect your health and your floor. |
| No subsoil drains around the house | Subsoil drains (pipes around the house) lead ground water away. Without working drains, water soaks the foundation and the basement. | Working drains keep your foundation and basement dry. They help prevent mould. |
| Blocked wall ventilation gaps in a brick-clad wall | A brick-clad wall needs a gap at the bottom. Air in the gap dries the wall. Mortar or dirt can block the gap. Then the wall stays wet and the wood behind it can rot. | Open gaps let the wall dry. They protect the wall frame. |
| Poor surface drainage next to the house | Rain should flow away from your house. If the ground slopes toward the wall, or a downspout ends too close, water soaks the foundation. | A dry foundation stays strong. You prevent damp and mould in the basement. |
| Worn sealant in the wet room | Sealant (silicone) in the shower corner and around the floor drain wears out. Then water can slip behind the tiles. | New sealant keeps water out. It is a low-cost job that protects the floor. |
| Wet-room waterproofing past its service life | A waterproof layer from 1999 to 2005 is now over 20 years old. It may start to fail. The damage stays hidden under the tiles. | An inspector tells you if the layer still works. You renew it only when needed. |
| Cracked sealant at a balcony or terrace door | Rain can enter under a cracked door threshold. Water can then rot the floor edge. | New sealant keeps rain out of the floor and the wall. |
| Sewer backflow protection | In heavy rain, sewer water can flow back into a basement through a floor drain. A backflow valve stops it. A pump needs an alarm, so you know if it fails. | You keep your basement dry in heavy rain. |

## 6. Plumbing

| Risk | Why it matters | What you gain |
|---|---|---|
| Cast-iron floor drain in the wet room | An old cast-iron drain does not seal well to a modern waterproof layer. Water can leak around the drain into the floor. Floor drains cause many leaks. | A new drain, sealed to the waterproof layer, keeps water out of the floor. |
| Pipe renovation overdue in a 1960s to 1980s flat | The pipes in buildings from this time are at the end of their life. A leak can flood your flat and the flat below. | A pipe survey and a planned renovation prevent a sudden leak. They also protect the value of your home. |
| Copper water pipes over 40 years old | Copper pipes get tiny holes from the inside after about 40 years. A pipe in the floor can leak where you cannot see it. | New pipes remove a hidden leak source. |
| Galvanized steel water pipes | Steel pipes with a zinc coating rust from the inside. After about 40 years they leak. | New pipes remove one of the biggest leak sources in your home. |
| Hot-water tank past its service life | A hot-water tank rusts from the inside. After about 20 years it can leak with no warning. | A new tank, or a drip tray with a leak alarm, stops a slow leak from soaking your floor. |
| Cast-iron sewer pipes over 50 years old | Cast-iron sewer pipes rust and crack after about 50 years. Waste water can then leak into the building. | A pipe check shows the real condition. You plan a repair before a leak. |
| No leak tray under the dishwasher or washing machine | A dishwasher or a washing machine can leak slowly. Without a tray, the water soaks the floor, often out of sight. | A tray catches the water. You see the leak before it does damage. |
| Old washing machine hoses or no own shut-off valve | An old rubber hose can burst. A burst hose can flood your home in minutes. A separate shut-off valve lets you stop the water. | New hoses and a valve cut the risk of a flood from your washing machine. |
| No leak detector or automatic shut-off valve | Most leaks start small. You may not see them for days. A detector gives an alarm. An automatic valve stops the water at once. | You find a leak early and limit the damage. |
| Water not shut off during long absences | A leak in an empty home runs for days before anyone sees it. The damage grows each day. | Closing the main valve, or a sensor with an alarm, protects the home while you are away. |

## 7. Roof and building envelope

| Risk | Why it matters | What you gain |
|---|---|---|
| Bitumen felt roof over 25 years old | Bitumen felt (a black roof covering) wears out in 20 to 30 years. It leaks first at the seams. Water then wets the attic insulation. | A roof check or a new covering keeps rain out of your attic and ceiling. |
| Steel sheet roof over 40 years old | A steel roof leaks first at the screws, the seams and the edges. Your roof is old and has no repair on record. | A check and new screws can add 10 to 15 years to your roof. This costs much less than a new roof. |
| Tile roof underlay over 30 years old | Roof tiles last a long time. The sheet under the tiles does not. After about 30 years it lets wind-driven rain and melt water through. | A new underlay keeps water out of your attic. You keep your tiles. |
| Roof drains and gutters not cleaned each year | Leaves block roof drains and gutters. Water then pools on the roof or runs down the wall. This wears the roof and wets the wall. | Clean drains keep water off your roof and walls. It takes little time. |
| Low-pitch roof without a snow-load plan | Heavy snow on a flat or low roof adds a lot of weight. In a hard winter, the roof can leak or fail. | A plan for snow removal protects your roof. You know when to act. |
| Tall trees that can fall on the house | A tall tree that is old or sick can fall in a storm. It can hit your roof, your wall or your car. | A tree service checks the tree and removes the danger before a storm. |
| Windows at the end of their service life | Old windows let in cold air. Hard seals let in drafts and rain. Wood frames can rot. | New seals or new windows cut your heating bill and keep the wood dry. |
| Wood cladding needs maintenance | Paint protects wood from rain. When the paint wears off, the wood soaks up water and can rot. | Fresh paint keeps your wall dry and lasts for years. |
| Terrace needs maintenance | A wood terrace turns grey and can rot if you do not treat it. Rot weakens the boards and the frame. | A coat of oil or stain keeps the terrace safe and in good shape. |
| Wild animals in the roof or walls | Martens, squirrels and bats can enter through small gaps. They chew wires and insulation and leave dirt. | Mesh over the gaps keeps animals out. You protect your wires and insulation. |

## 8. Ventilation

| Risk | Why it matters | What you gain |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanical ventilation unit not serviced in 2 years | Filters clog and fans get dirty. The air in your home gets worse and the unit uses more power. | A service keeps the air fresh and lowers your heating bill. |
| Natural ventilation in a house from before 2000 | A tight home with new windows does not get enough air by itself. Moisture from showers stays in the home and can cause mould. | A fan in the bathroom dries the room after each shower and keeps mould away. |
| Supply-air valve blocked | Your ventilation pulls air out. New air must come in through the supply-air valves (small vents). If they are blocked, cold air pulls in through cracks and moisture stays. | Open valves bring in fresh air without drafts and keep moisture low. |

## 9. Indoor air

| Risk | Why it matters | What you gain |
|---|---|---|
| Radon not measured | Radon is a natural gas from the ground. It can enter a home through the floor. Over many years it can harm your lungs. Some areas have more radon than others. | A low-cost measurement shows if your home is safe. If it is not, a fix lowers the level. |
| Asbestos and PAH survey missing before a renovation | Buildings from before 1994 can contain asbestos (a fibre that harms the lungs) and PAH (harmful tar chemicals). Renovation work can release them. | A survey before the work protects you and the workers. It stops harmful dust from spreading. |

## 10. Building and documents

| Risk | Why it matters | What you gain |
|---|---|---|
| Building or extension without a permit | A building or an extension without a permit can cause trouble. The municipality can ask you to change it or remove it. Some insurance cover may not apply. | A check shows if your papers are in order. You can fix gaps before you sell the home or have a damage. |
| Wet-room or electrical work without documents | Wet-room work and electrical work done without papers can hide mistakes. These mistakes cause water damage and fires. Without papers, nobody can show that the work was safe. | An expert checks the work and writes a report. You get proof for your records and for a buyer. |

## 11. Security

| Risk | Why it matters | What you gain |
|---|---|---|
| Weak locks and no alarm | Burglars choose homes with weak doors and no alarm. Your insurance asks for locks that meet a standard. | Good locks and an alarm lower the risk of a break-in. An alarm that limits the damage can also lower your deductible (the part of a claim you pay). |

## 12. About your building (housing company items)

These items have no score. The customer cannot fix them alone. The app shows them in the list "about your building".

| Item | Why it matters | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Housing company renovation costs | Your housing company plans big repairs, for example the facade or the balcony. You pay your share through your monthly charge. | Plan the cost early. Ask the housing company for the dates and the amounts. |
| Shared heat pump near the end of its service life | The shared heat pump is old. A new part can cost the housing company a lot. You pay your share. | Know that the cost may come. Ask for the housing company's maintenance plan. |

## 13. Facts, assumptions and unknowns

### Facts
- The 57 risks and the 2 housing company items are from `docs/cataloguereview.md` and `docs/missingrisks.md`.
- The text follows the rules in section 1.

### Assumptions
- The text is in English. Translation to Finnish and Swedish comes in phase 3.
- The statements "sauna stoves are one of the main causes of electrical fires" and "the law asks for a yearly chimney sweep" come from the old DRAFT hints and the If terms (clause 5.2.4). I did not check them again.
- The statement "an alarm that limits the damage can lower your deductible" comes from the If terms (clause 6.4.1).

### Unknowns
- Whether the text works for older readers. Test it with 2 or 3 people from the target group.
- Whether the Finnish translation keeps the plain tone. Check with a native speaker in phase 3.
- Whether the text for advice-only risks (backup heat, snow load plan, roof drains, radon, asbestos) needs a different label in the app.
