The moment

Owning an older home is a life event that many people meet without a guide. They buy a house, plan to sell it, or read an inspection report that says "much to repair" (paljon korjattavaa). The report is long and written for experts. The owner does not know what matters, whom to call, or what waiting will cost.

Insurance is often visible only after the damage. A fire from an old fuse box or a water damage from old pipes is the moment the customer meets If. We want If to be there before.

The idea

The customer uploads a home inspection report. The app turns it into a short list of risks, each with a reason in plain words, a benefit, and a quote from the customer's own report. The customer books an If-vetted partner. The partner confirms the fix. The care score rises and unlocks rewards.

The demo in one line

A 68-year-old owner of a 1975 house in Turku uploads a report that says "much to repair". The app shows 5 risks. The owner books an electrician for the old screw-fuse panel. The electrician confirms. The score goes from 486 to 546 and unlocks Silver: 5% off the next job and offers on If products.

Team: Antton (system design, backend, model pipeline), Ege (app design), Rasi (risk analysis and service design). All partners, prices and score numbers in the demo are mock data. The mechanism is real.
Criterion 1

Customer value and relevance

The need is real and easy to state: "Tell me what to fix first, who can do it, and what I gain." An owner of an older house should not need to read a long technical report or search for a contractor.

What the customer gets

  • A short list. Up to 5 risks. The app ranks them by severity × probability, so the most serious come first.
  • A reason in plain words. Each risk says why it matters and what the customer gains. No expert terms.
  • Proof. Each risk shows the sentence from the customer's own report, with the page number.
  • Partners that If has vetted. Rating, price range, If-vetted badge and the customer's discount on one card.
  • A reward ladder. Four tiers: Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum.
  • A maintenance list. Each check has a due date and a state: overdue, due soon or scheduled.
  • Family help. A relative can act for the customer, with the customer's permission.

Built for the average Finn, including older people

A native app, simple screens, plain words. Finnish, Swedish and English. The customer never has to learn insurance terms.

What the customer protects

Their safety, because fire, oil leaks and water damage are the risks that matter most. Their money, because a fix costs less than the damage. Their insurance position, because the If terms ask for repaired electrical faults and approved installers, and cut payments by the age of old electrical equipment. A new panel restarts that age.

Only outside proof moves the score. A customer's answers alone cannot take them past Bronze (about 450). False information sets the benefit score to 0. Old fixes fade, so the customer keeps up the care.

Why this is credible

  • If already runs a related programme, Kunnon Koti: a partner inspects detached houses every four years. It gives a one-off report with no effect on the premium. If Life Partner makes this continuous, scored and rewarded.
  • Press coverage of If's own inspections reports that about one in five houses has no subsoil drains, or nobody knows if they exist (Taloustaito). That is a risk If can see and reduce.
  • The mechanism does not need new insurance products. It works on top of the existing home insurance and its terms.
Criterion 3

Feasibility and scalability

The backend is built and the app is in design. The design rule keeps it safe to scale: code decides, models read and write.

How it works in practice

  • Input: a standard Finnish inspection report (kuntotarkastus, KH 90-00394). The format is the same across inspectors, so one reader works for all.
  • A model extracts the facts: year, heating, panel, pipes, roof. Every fact keeps its source, a quote and a confidence.
  • Code matches the facts to a risk catalogue. The catalogue holds severity, probability, points, partner type and re-check interval. A model cannot change them. It may only choose catalogue ids.
  • A model writes the customer text from catalogue hints. A person can review every text.
  • Code picks the partners and builds the maintenance list.

The model runs on local hardware. This keeps customer documents private and keeps the cost low.

How it scales

  • More risks, no new engine. A risk is one catalogue entry. The 38 new risks we designed from If's terms need new entries and a few new house facts, not new code.
  • More possessions. A car or a life event, for example a marriage or a new child, is a new catalogue and a new score. The care score combines them.
  • More partners. A partner joins by vetting and listing. We defined 14 partner types and the proof each one gives.
  • More data. Public data, for example the building register and weather alerts, feeds the maintenance list.
  • More languages. The catalogue text is plain and short, so it translates well into Finnish and Swedish.

Criterion 4

Innovation and experience

Most insurance apps show policies and claims. If Life Partner does not. Insurance is not the centre of the experience. The customer's home is.

From claim time to care time

The service meets the customer when they take responsibility for an ageing home. It helps with the work itself: what to fix, who does it, what it gives back. If becomes a partner in the home, not a bill and a claim form.

Proof, not promises

The care score moves only when an outside source confirms a fix: a partner, an inspector or a document. The customer cannot fake it. That makes the score useful to If and trustworthy to the customer.

Insurance terms as a to-do list

The home insurance terms hold protection rules: sweep the chimney, test smoke alarms, close the main valve after a week away. Few customers read them. We turn each rule into a risk and a task, so the terms protect the customer in practice.

Every risk traces to the customer's own document

The app shows the quote and the page. The customer sees that the risk is not invented. Facts show their source, and unknown facts are marked as unknown.

The experience

  • Short and calm. Five risks, not fifty. Plain words, no alarm.
  • Gamified with real rewards. A score from 0 to 1000, four metal tiers, and a bar that shows the next reward.
  • A reason to return. Seasonal reminders, a maintenance list, and a score that fades without upkeep.
  • Family-ready. A relative can follow and act, so the oldest customers are not left out.
