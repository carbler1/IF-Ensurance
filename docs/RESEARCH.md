# If Life Partner: home risk assessment

Status: draft, 2026-10-17. Updated with the If home insurance terms. Not yet reviewed in interview.
Scope: this document lists the hazard categories for each home type. Market: Finland.
Not in scope: prices and proof of work. These come later.
Source of the terms: If "Kodin Omaisuusvakuutusehdot", term OMA 200.27, valid from 1 January 2026 (file `kotivakuutusehdot.pdf`).

## 1. Summary

A hazard is a source of possible damage to a home or its occupants. A hazard category is a group of related hazards.

The app asks the customer for the home type. The home type decides which hazard categories the app shows. This document covers three home types:

- Detached house (omakotitalo): one building for one household, on its own plot.
- Row house (rivitalo): a row of connected houses. Each household owns its own unit. The units share walls.
- Apartment (kerrostalo or apartment building): a unit in a larger building. A housing company (asunto-osakeyhtiö) owns the building. The customer owns shares that give the right to live in the unit.

This version adds every risk that the If terms name. The terms name four kinds of risk:

1. Insured events (terms clause 3): the damage that the insurance pays for.
2. Excluded events (clause 4): damage that the insurance does not pay for. These are risks the customer carries alone.
3. Protection rules (clause 5): rules the customer must follow. A broken rule can reduce or remove the payment.
4. Payment rules (clause 6): rules that reduce the payment or cause a gap, for example age deductions.

Section 7 maps each clause to a category in the tables. Facts, assumptions and unknowns are in section 8.

## 2. How to read the tables

- **Who controls it** shows who can reduce the hazard:
  - Owner: the customer.
  - Housing company: the housing company (taloyhtiö) and its manager.
  - Shared: both the customer and the housing company.
- **Cover** shows what the If terms do with the hazard. The number is the clause in the terms (for example 3.5 is clause 3.5):
  - Covered: the insurance pays for sudden and unforeseen damage.
  - Limited: the insurance pays, but with a limit, a deduction or a condition.
  - Not covered: the insurance does not pay. The customer carries the loss.
  - Rule: a protection rule. A broken rule can reduce or remove the payment.
  - Not in terms: the hazard is real, but the terms do not name it.
- **Likelihood** shows how likely the hazard is to cause a loss in Finland. It is a first estimate. Values: High, Medium, Low.
- **Severity** shows how much harm the hazard causes when it happens. It covers the cost of the damage and the risk to people. It is a first estimate. Values: extremely low, low, medium, high, critical. A critical hazard can threaten life or make the home unlivable.
- **Mitigations** are actions that reduce the hazard or the cost of a claim. The customer or the housing company does them. Where the housing company controls the hazard, the customer asks for the action and reports faults.
- A hazard that is in the tables is relevant to that home type. A hazard that is not in the tables is not relevant, or it is not the customer's responsibility.

Words from the terms:
- Sudden and unforeseen damage (äkillinen ja ennalta arvaamaton vahinko): damage that happens fast, with no warning signs, and that normal care could not prevent.
- Deductible (omavastuu): the part of each claim that the customer pays.
- Protection rule (suojeluohje): a rule in clause 5 of the terms.

## 4. Row house

The customer owns one unit. The unit shares walls with neighbours. The housing company or a similar body often owns the common parts, but this changes between buildings. The app must ask who owns the roof, the outer walls and the yard. The terms also say that a claim is not paid when the housing company must also pay for the customer's own parts (4.26). The terms pay for a damage inside the customer's unit only (2.6).

## 5. Apartment

The customer owns shares in the housing company. The housing company owns and maintains the building structure, the pipes in the walls and the common parts. The customer is usually responsible for the surfaces and fixtures inside the unit. The exact limit is in the housing company's articles of association (yhtiöjärjestys). This limit decides who pays for a damage. The app must show it to the customer. The terms pay for damage inside the insured unit only (2.6), and not when the housing company must also pay (4.26).

## 8. Facts, assumptions and unknowns

### Facts
- The If terms (OMA 200.27, valid from 1 January 2026) name the risks in section 7. Cover and rules in the tables come from the terms.
- Water damage is a major source of home insurance claims in Finland. *(Source to add.)*
- The housing company owns the building structure and common parts in an apartment building. This is the normal rule under Finnish housing company law. The articles of association can change the detail.
- The terms pay for an exceptional flood only. Heavy rain is exceptional at 30 mm in 1 hour or 75 mm in 1 day. A water level is exceptional at once in 50 years or rarer (terms, definitions).

### Assumptions
- The likelihood and severity values in sections 3, 4 and 5 are first estimates. The team has not checked them against If claim data.
- The hazard lists cover the average Finnish home. Special cases, for example a holiday home (mökki) or a wooden apartment building, are not covered.
- The customer can answer the home type question without help.
- The row house and apartment cover columns use the same terms as the detached house. The terms do not give a separate rule for each home type, except clause 2.6 and 4.26 for apartments and similar units.

### Unknowns
- Which hazards If can verify with outside proof, as the service spec requires.
- Which of the hazards give the best claim reduction per mitigation.
- Which hazards the customer cannot mitigate. The team may remove these. Examples: public network failure and building structure faults.
- How to treat the border cases: a semi-detached house (paritalo), a townhouse with a shared housing company, and a holiday home.
- How the app gets the apartment ownership limit for each housing company.
- Which deductible and cover choices a customer has in the policy letter (vakuutuskirja). The terms refer to it but do not list it.
